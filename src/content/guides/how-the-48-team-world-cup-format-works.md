The men's World Cup grew from 32 to 48 teams for the 2026 tournament, hosted jointly by the United States, Canada and Mexico. The new World Cup format needed a different structure, and a few details matter if you want to follow the tournament or predict its scores. This guide explains how the stages fit together and what changed compared with the older design.

## The structure at a glance

The tournament has two phases:

1. **Group stage.** Forty-eight teams are drawn into twelve groups of four. Each team plays the other three once, so every team has exactly three group matches.
2. **Knockout stage.** Thirty-two teams remain, and they play single-match rounds: a round of 32, a round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals, a third-place match and the final.

## Who goes through from the groups

Twelve groups send two teams each, which makes 24 qualifiers. The remaining eight places go to the best third-placed teams. Third-placed teams from all twelve groups are ranked in a single table using the tournament's tiebreakers, and the top eight advance. Because those teams come from different groups and have played different opponents, goal difference and goals scored often decide the order.

This design has a notable effect: a team can lose a group match and still go through, and a third-placed team can miss out depending on how the other groups finish. Knowing the likely cut-off helps explain why teams chase goals in the final group matches.

## Why a round of 32

With 32 survivors, the knockout bracket needs an extra round. The round of 32 is the first one-match knockout, and it means every team that reaches the final plays eight matches in total: three in the group and five in the knockout rounds. In the 32-team format, finalists played seven.

## The numbers compared

| | 32-team format | 48-team format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teams | 32 | 48 |
| Groups | 8 groups of 4 | 12 groups of 4 |
| Advance from groups | 16 | 32 |
| Total matches | 64 | 104 |
| Matches for a finalist | 7 | 8 |

## Extra time and penalties

Knockout matches that are level after 90 minutes go to extra time and then a penalty shootout. The procedure is described in the guide to [extra time and penalty shootouts](/guides/extra-time-and-penalty-shootouts-explained). In a score prediction game that grades the 90-minute result, extra time is ignored, as it is on this site's [prediction page](/predict).

## What the format means for predictions

A bigger field and a softer group stage change the rhythm of the tournament in a few ways:

- **More mismatches early.** With 48 teams, some group matches pair established sides with much weaker teams. Where the rating gap, explained in the [Elo guide](/guides/elo-ratings-in-soccer-explained), is large, lopsided results become more likely.
- **Fewer dead matches.** With eight third-placed teams going through, more teams may stay alive until the final group round, which can mean more motivated matches.
- **Draws matter in the table.** A draw earns one point, and the [tiebreaker rules](/guides/soccer-league-table-tiebreakers-explained) rank third-placed teams, so a goalless draw can decide who advances.
- **More matches for deep runs.** A team that reaches the final stages can now play more games, which also helps individual records: FIFA noted that Kylian Mbappé benefited from eight matches at the 48-team tournament, as the [list of World Cup top scorers](/guides/world-cup-all-time-top-scorers) shows.
- **Neutral venues.** Most matches are not at a team's home, so the [home advantage](/guides/home-advantage-in-soccer-how-big-is-it) adjustment usually applies only to the host nations.

## How the draw keeps strong teams apart

Teams are placed into pots before the draw, and each group takes one team from each pot, so the highest-ranked sides do not share a group. Seeding has relied on the FIFA ranking, whose method is compared with Elo in the guide to the [FIFA ranking and Elo ratings](/guides/fifa-world-ranking-vs-elo-ratings-difference). The three hosts are placed in fixed groups so that they play their opening matches at home. The draw also applies geographic limits so that teams from the same confederation are separated where the rules require it.

## A group of four in practice

Each group has six matches, played over three rounds. After two rounds many teams know whether they are close to safe, which is why the final round is played at the same time in each group. Simultaneous kickoffs stop one side from adjusting its approach based on the other match. For predictions, the final round is where motivation matters most, but it should stay a small adjustment and not become the headline reason for a pick.

## Comparing with the Champions League

The Champions League league phase uses a single table of 36 clubs instead of groups, which creates different incentives. See [how the new Champions League format works](/guides/how-the-new-champions-league-format-works) for the comparison.

## Where to read the official rules

FIFA publishes the competition regulations, including the draw procedure and tiebreakers, on [fifa.com](https://www.fifa.com). Details such as the exact order of group tiebreakers are set in those regulations and can differ from other competitions, so read the current edition before relying on a specific rule.
