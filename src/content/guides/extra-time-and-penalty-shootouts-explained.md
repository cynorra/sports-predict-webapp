Knockout football has a rule that league football does not need: someone must advance. When 90 minutes end level, competitions use a ladder of extra steps to find a winner. The details come from the Laws of the Game and the rules of each tournament, and they matter for predictions because the "result" of a match can mean different things depending on which stage you count. This guide lays the sequence out in order.

## Step one: 90 minutes plus stoppage time

A normal match has two halves of 45 minutes, plus added time for stoppages that the referee announces. If one side leads at the end, the tie is over. If the score is level and the competition needs a winner, play moves on.

## Step two: extra time

Extra time consists of two periods of 15 minutes, with a brief change of ends in between. The teams get a short rest before it starts. Competitions may allow an additional substitution once extra time begins. Extra time is always played in full: the old "golden goal" and "silver goal" variants that ended a match early were dropped from the rules years ago.

Some competitions skip extra time altogether. Certain cup rounds go straight from 90 minutes to penalties, and some two-legged ties use extra time only after the second leg.

## Step three: the penalty shootout

A shootout is not part of the match score. It is a separate procedure used to decide a winner, and goals scored in it are not added to the match result. The sequence is:

1. A coin toss decides which team takes the first kick.
2. Teams alternate kicks, five each.
3. The shootout can end early if one team cannot be caught by the other even if all its remaining kicks are scored.
4. If the score is level after five kicks each, the shootout continues in rounds of one kick per team until one team scores and the other does not in the same round.

Only players who were on the pitch at the end of the match can take a kick, and a team that has substitutions left may replace an injured goalkeeper. In sudden death, every eligible player on a team must have taken a kick before any of them takes a second one.

## Two-legged ties and the away-goals rule

For many years a level two-leg tie was settled first by the away-goals rule: away goals counted double if the aggregate was level. UEFA removed the rule in its club competitions from the 2021-22 season. Today a level aggregate leads to extra time and then penalties. Domestic cups and other tournaments may still use different arrangements, so confirm the rules for a given competition.

## How the result is counted in a prediction game

This matters for anyone predicting scores. Different organisations treat the final result differently:

- A match "result" may mean the score after 90 minutes.
- It may mean the score after extra time.
- It may mean who advanced, including the shootout.

On this site's [prediction page](/predict), picks are scored on the result after 90 minutes plus stoppage time, so extra time and penalties are ignored. That keeps scoring consistent with how league matches are judged and with the [model](/methodology), which forecasts the 90-minute score. ## Common questions about the shootout

**Who takes the kicks?** Any eligible player can be chosen, and the order is chosen by the coach. The goalkeeper may take a kick like any other player.

**What if a team has fewer players than the other?** If a team finishes the match with fewer players, the other team reduces its list to match, so both sides take the same number of kicks.

## What shootouts tell us about prediction

Penalty shootouts feel like pure drama, but they follow the same logic as the rest of football: small samples, big noise. A penalty kick is scored far more often than not, so a shootout is usually decided by narrow margins and a little luck. Reading too much into one shootout is a classic small-sample mistake. For a deeper look at why, see the guide on [form, luck and small samples](/guides/small-samples-and-football-form-luck-or-skill).

## Where to read the exact rules

The Laws of the Game are published by IFAB, which also describes the standard penalty shootout procedure; the full text is on the [IFAB website](https://www.theifab.com). Each tournament adds its own regulations on extra time and substitutes, so check those before relying on a detail.
