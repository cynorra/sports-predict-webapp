The most goals in a Premier League season is 36, scored by Erling Haaland for Manchester City in 2022-23. He took the record from Alan Shearer and Andrew Cole, who each scored 34 when a season still had 42 matches. This guide lists the highest single-season totals, explains why the move to 38 games matters when you compare eras, and gives the career record that Shearer still holds.

The figures here come from the Premier League's own records page, ESPN's list of the highest season totals and the news report from the match in which the record fell.

## The record: 36 goals in a 38-game season

Haaland's 36 goals came in 35 appearances, according to ESPN, in his first season in English football. He equalled the old mark of 34 at Fulham and then passed it with his 35th league goal in a 3-0 win over West Ham on 3 May 2023, a record-setting goal that Al Jazeera reported with his team-mates forming a guard of honour. At that point he still had five league matches left, and he finished on 36.

The Premier League's records page lists the same figure twice, as the most goals in a season and as the most goals in a debut Premier League season. Nobody has scored more in their first season in the competition.

## Why 34 stood for so long

Shearer and Cole set the old record in an earlier version of the league. ESPN and Al Jazeera both point out that their 34 came before the league was reduced from 22 clubs to 20 in 1995, so each played 42 matches, four more than a team plays today. Cole scored his 34 for Newcastle United in 1993-94 and Shearer his for Blackburn Rovers in 1994-95.

Haaland therefore had four fewer matches available than Shearer and Cole when he passed a mark that had stood for almost three decades. The shorter season makes his total more impressive in one sense, since he had fewer chances, and it does not erase the older totals, which are simply a different format to compare against.

## The top single-season totals

ESPN lists the highest totals, with an asterisk for 42-game seasons:

- 36: Erling Haaland, Manchester City, 2022-23
- 34: Alan Shearer, Blackburn Rovers, 1994-95 (42 games)
- 34: Andrew Cole, Newcastle United, 1993-94 (42 games)
- 32: Mohamed Salah, Liverpool, 2017-18
- 31: Luis Suárez, Liverpool, 2013-14
- 31: Cristiano Ronaldo, Manchester United, 2007-08
- 31: Alan Shearer, Blackburn Rovers, 1995-96
- 31: Alan Shearer, Blackburn Rovers, 1993-94 (42 games)

Three of the eight entries come from 42-game seasons, and Shearer appears three times. His 1995-96 total of 31 is notable because it was scored in a 38-game season, the same format Haaland, Salah, Suárez and Ronaldo played in.

## Home-goal records

The Premier League's records page also lists the most goals scored at home in a season. Shearer holds it with 25 for Blackburn Rovers in 1994/95, in 21 home matches. The page also credits Haaland with 22 for Manchester City in 2023/24, in 19 home matches. Both entries are worth more than a goal per home game on average.

## The career record: Alan Shearer's 260

The league's all-time top scorer is Alan Shearer with 260 goals for Blackburn Rovers and Newcastle United between 1992/93 and 2005/06, according to the Premier League's records page. That page lists only the leader. Career totals for active players change every weekend, so check the Premier League's [records page](https://www.premierleague.com/en/stats/records) for the current list rather than relying on a fixed table. ## What a record season says about scoring

A player who scores close to a goal per match across a whole season is far out in the tail of any scoring distribution. A typical team scores only one or two goals in a match, and the likeliest scorelines are narrow, as the guide to the [Poisson distribution](/guides/poisson-distribution-football-goals-explained) shows. That is why a statistical model treats a 36-goal season as an outlier to admire, not a pattern to forecast.

One more caution for readers who compare strikers: raw goals ignore chance quality. The guide to [expected goals](/guides/what-is-xg-expected-goals-explained) explains how a scorer can run above or below the quality of his chances, and why records built on high finishing rates are hard to repeat.
