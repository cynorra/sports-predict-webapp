Cards are football's way of signalling discipline. A yellow card warns a player, a red card ends their match. The basic idea is easy, but the details cause arguments: which offences earn which card, what a team loses after a sending-off and how suspensions carry into later matches. This guide sets out the rules, with the Laws of the Game as the source.

## The yellow card

A yellow card is a caution. According to the Laws, a player is cautioned for offences such as:

- unsporting behaviour, including reckless tackles and simulation,
- dissent by word or action,
- persistently committing offences,
- delaying the restart of play,
- failing to retreat the required distance at a free kick, corner kick or throw-in,
- entering, re-entering or leaving the pitch without the referee's permission.

A caution does not remove a player from the match. A second caution in the same match leads to a red card.

## The red card

A red card means a sending-off. A player can be sent off in two ways:

1. **A second caution** in the same match.
2. **A direct red card** for one of these offences:
   - serious foul play,
   - violent conduct,
   - spitting at someone,
   - denying the opposing team a goal or an obvious goal-scoring opportunity by deliberately handling the ball (except a goalkeeper in their own penalty area),
   - denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity to an opponent moving towards the player's goal by an offence punishable by a free kick or penalty,
   - using offensive, insulting or abusive language or gestures.

A player who is sent off must leave the pitch and its surroundings. The team cannot replace them, so it plays the remainder of the match with one player fewer.

## What a sending-off does to a match

Playing with ten players is a real handicap, though the effect depends on timing. An early red card makes the match very different, while a late one changes little. Teams down to ten often drop deeper and defend a lead or a draw. Their expected goals tend to fall, and the opponent's tend to rise. No model can foresee a red card, so it is a source of uncertainty in every forecast, much like the random swings discussed in the guide to [form and luck](/guides/small-samples-and-football-form-luck-or-skill).

## Suspensions

A red card carries an automatic suspension from at least the next match. The length depends on the offence and the competition. Serious cases, such as violent conduct, can bring longer bans decided by a disciplinary body. Yellow cards also matter beyond a single match: many competitions suspend a player after a set number of yellows, and the threshold differs by league and tournament. In knockout tournaments cautions are sometimes wiped after a stage so that players do not miss a final for earlier offences. The only reliable source is the competition's own disciplinary regulations.

## Cards for non-players

Substitutes, substituted players and team officials can also be cautioned or sent off. A person who is sent off must leave the technical area and the surroundings of the pitch.

## Cards and video review

A direct red card is one of the four situations the video assistant referee may check, as set out in the guide to [VAR](/guides/var-in-football-explained-what-it-can-review). A second yellow card is not reviewable, which is one reason it often causes argument. The one exception is mistaken identity, when the wrong player is shown a card.

## Misunderstandings about cards

**"A foul from behind is always a red."** Not by itself. The referee looks at the force, the danger to the opponent and whether the offence stopped an obvious goal-scoring chance.

**"A tactical foul is free."** A deliberate foul to stop a promising attack is punished by a caution, and by a red card if it denies an obvious goal-scoring opportunity.

**"Yellow cards carry over forever."** Within one match they count towards a sending-off, and across matches they count only under the competition's accumulation rules, which differ between leagues and tournaments.

**"The captain can argue with the referee."** The captain has a special role in communicating with the referee, but dissent by anyone, including the captain, can still be cautioned.

Cards are one of the few sources of randomness that change a match after the lineups are known.
